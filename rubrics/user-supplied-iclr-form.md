# User-supplied ICLR-style review form

Provenance: pasted by the author in the conversation. Exact official conference year/form has not been independently verified. Preserve these fields and anchors without substituting a historical scale. Development-mode null/not-assessable values are a tool extension, NOT part of this form. An author-facing improvement plan is separate from the form.

## Paper Summary*
Please concisely summarize the paper, contributions, and validation as you understand them to be.

## Presentation*
Is the paper clearly presented?

1: Difficult to follow despite careful reading.
2: Generally understandable, but requires substantial effort in places.
3: Clear and easy to follow.

## Informativeness*
Is the paper informative?

1: I learned little or nothing new from this paper.
2: I learned something interesting or useful.
3: I gained a substantially deeper understanding of the problem or question.

## Soundness*
Is the paper sound?

1: I have significant concerns about the paper's soundness that do not appear easy to address.
2: I have minor or fixable concerns about the paper's soundness.
3: I have no substantive concerns about the paper's soundness.

## Critical Strengths*
Please identify one or two critical strengths of the paper that support acceptance. For each, explain briefly why it is consequential to your overall assessment. The goal is not to enumerate every positive point, but to identify and substantiate the considerations that most strongly determine your recommendation. Enter "None" if no substantive strength can be identified.

## Critical Weaknesses*
Please identify one or two critical weaknesses of the paper that support rejection. For each, explain briefly why it is consequential to your overall assessment. The goal is not to enumerate every negative point, but to identify and substantiate the considerations that most strongly determine your recommendation. Enter "None" if no material weakness can be identified.

## Questions For Authors*
Ask only questions whose answers could materially affect your assessment. Distinguish clarification requests from suggestions for future work.

## Ethics Review
Please consider whether the submission raises any ethics concerns, including

- discrimination/bias/fairness;
- privacy, security, or safety;
- legal compliance (e.g., GDPR, copyright, terms of use);
- potentially harmful insights, methodologies, or applications;
- responsible research practice (e.g., human subjects, data release);
- research integrity (e.g., plagiarism, dual submission);
- unprofessional behavior;
- or other ethics-related concerns.

Yes / No

## Details Of Ethics Concerns
If you selected “Yes” for Ethics Review, please briefly describe the concern(s).

## Rating*
Based on your overall assessment of the submission, what is your recommended decision? Consider the paper's overall soundness, significance, clarity, and contribution.

1: Clear rejection
2: Weak rejection
3: Weak acceptance
4: Clear acceptance

## Confidence*
How strong is your subject-matter basis for this recommendation? Indicate your level of familiarity and research experience with the specific topic of the paper.

1: I do not have sufficient expertise to assess this submission reliably and have alerted the AC.
2: I have broad familiarity with this field but little or no direct research experience on this topic.
3: I am familiar with this topic and its relevant literature.
4: I have substantial direct research experience on this topic.

## Tool representation (outside the supplied form)
Structured results use `ethics_review.answer` (`yes` or `no`) and `ethics_review.details`; a yes answer requires specific details. `confidence` contains a score and rationale. For an AI reviewer, disclose that confidence is a self-assessed proxy based on available evidence and topic coverage, not human research experience; never claim to have alerted an AC. These qualifications do not change the supplied anchors or establish an official venue-year form.
